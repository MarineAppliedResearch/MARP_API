How MARP works

A survey takes a day, and turning it into science can take months. This is where those months go, and what MARP does about it.

Where the months go

Recording the survey is the fast part.

The slow part begins afterward.

Watching the footage back, working out what is in it, checking the result, getting it ready for whoever needs it next. That time grows with every hour of imagery a survey brings home.

Why the workflow is narrow on purpose

A system that can do anything has to be told everything.

Software that could support any scientific process has to be told which files belong together and what happens to them next. Every project answers that from scratch.

MARP is built around one sequence of work, so the answers are already in it. Video belongs to a dive. A candidate stays a proposal until a biologist has looked at it.

That means a biologist can sit down and work instead of configuring a workflow first.

Underneath

Everything learned from a survey stays with the survey.

The footage, where it was recorded, what the models found, what biologists accepted or corrected, and the analyses built from that work all stay connected. As the work advances, the scientific picture can start taking shape instead of being assembled again at the very end.

Reports & Data Products

One thing this bought us

Every observation leads back to the exact frame.

MARP's player lets a reviewer move through footage frame by frame in either direction and return to the exact moment behind an observation. That keeps the scientific record tied to what the reviewer actually saw.

Who decides

Models make the first pass. Biologists make the result trustworthy.

Models can scan survey footage quickly and surface likely observations. Biologists review and correct those results, and that reviewed data helps train better models for the next round.
