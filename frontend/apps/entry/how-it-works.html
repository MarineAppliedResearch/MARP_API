How MARP works

A survey takes a day, and turning it into science can take months. This is where those months go, and what MARP does about it.

Where the months go

Recording it is the fast part.

A vehicle records for as long as it has power. Watching that back and working out what is in it has never scaled with the cameras. The bigger cost sits between the stages, in the work of getting from one of them to the next.

Every step rebuilds the last one's context

Video, navigation and observations get separated when they move between tools, then joined back up by hand on the other side. That rejoining work buys nothing. It gets you back to where the previous stage already was.

Nothing starts until the step before it finishes

Review waits on processing, and analysis waits on review. A stage can sit idle with plenty of work available to it, because the batch it has been told to wait for is still open.

The answers arrive after the questions moved on

A survey goes out to answer something. How much is out there, or whether it has changed since the last time anyone looked. The longer the answers take, the less of that question is still open when they arrive.

Why the workflow is narrow on purpose

A system that can do anything has to be told everything.

Software built to support any scientific process has to ask which files belong together and what happens to them next. Every project answers those questions again, and the answers end up in somebody's head or in a spreadsheet sitting next to the data.

MARP is built around one sequence of work, so most of them are already answered. Video belongs to a dive, a dive belongs to a project, and a detection stays a proposal until a biologist has looked at it. The application already knows all of that, which is what lets the interface stay small. It can fill things in and hand one stage to the next without being configured first.

The narrowness stops at the surface. Underneath are ordinary pieces, one API and one data model, and those are reusable anywhere somebody wants them. The workflow sitting on top is built for this job only, on purpose.

Underneath

Reports & Data Products

What comes out the far end, built from that same record.

The video, where the vehicle was, what a model proposed, what a biologist decided and what got corrected are the same data throughout. Nothing has to be rebuilt to move forward, and a correction made during review is the version everything downstream reads.

One thing this bought us

Going back one frame, as many times as it takes.

An analyst who spots something at a particular moment needs to get back to that exact frame, rather than to somewhere in its neighbourhood. A browser will not do that on its own. The video element refuses a negative playback rate outright, so reviewing footage in reverse becomes a series of backward seeks, and those land wherever the container's keyframes allow. Against streamed video that is usually not where the animal was.

So MARP has its own player. It fetches the media itself, demuxes the container, decodes with WebCodecs and draws to a canvas, which means it is holding the decoded frames and can put any one of them up on demand. Forwards or backwards, one frame at a time, for as long as somebody needs to keep checking.

A species identification rests on that. The observation is recorded against a frame, so anyone checking the work later can return to the same frame and see exactly what the first reviewer was looking at. The player is a MARP component in its own right, and the desktop annotation tool runs on it.

Who decides

Biologists lead. MARP amplifies.

A model can look at every frame of a survey, which no person has the hours to do. It cannot tell you whether what it found matters, and it is wrong often enough that treating its output as data would poison the record. So a detection is a proposal until a biologist accepts it, and the acceptance is recorded as that person's.

A detection is a proposal

Models put candidates in front of a reviewer. Nothing a model produced becomes an observation on its own.

The decision has a name on it

MARP records who reviewed what and what they decided, and keeps the earlier decisions instead of writing over them.

A correction goes upstream

Fixing an identification changes the record everything else reads. There is no second copy inside one application to go and update afterwards.

The dull half is the automated half

Pulling frames, matching timecodes, moving files, assembling the page in front of the reviewer. None of it needs a scientist.

Explore. Inform. Protect.

More of what a survey collects can turn into science, sooner. That is the whole of it.
